MANATHA FOUNDATION CONSTITUTION

Approved and accepted by members at a special (general) meeting held on 20 March 2024.

Certified as a true copy on 29 April 2025 by the Chief Directorate Non-Profit Organisations, Department of Social Development.

Download Constitution

1. Name

1.1 The organisation hereby constituted will be called: MANATHA FOUNDATION.

1.2 Its shortened name will be: MANATHA FOUNDATION (hereinafter referred to as the organisation).

2. Body Corporate

The organisation shall:

  • Exist in its own right, separately from its members.
  • Continue to exist even when its membership changes and there are different office bearers.
  • Be able to own property and other possessions.
  • Be able to sue and be sued in its own name.

3. Objectives

The organisation's main objectives are to:

  • Enhancing Well-being: Improve the overall well-being of the community by providing essential services and support systems to address immediate needs, such as food security, healthcare access, and other fundamental requirements.
  • Education and Career Development: Foster educational excellence and career development, empowering individuals, especially students in Grade 1 to 12, through mentorship, non-financial assistance, and guidance.
  • Digital Inclusion and Literacy: Bridge the digital divide by ensuring free internet access and promoting digital literacy initiatives.
  • Access to Education: Break down barriers to education by providing resources such as school uniforms and learning materials to children from low-income families.
  • Skills Development and Empowerment: Facilitate skills development through vocational training and entrepreneurship programs, enhancing self-sufficiency and economic growth.

4. Governing Structure and Mechanism of Governance

4.1 The Office Bearers will oversee the organisation. The Office Bearers will be made up of 3 members. They are the Board of governance of the organisation.

4.2 Office bearers will serve for a period agreed to by the general membership at an AGM, not exceeding three years. They can stand for re-election for another term in office again and again thereafter.

Vacancies: The Office Bearers must, as soon as reasonably possible, appoint someone to fill any vacancy that reduced the number of Office Bearers.

Resignation: An Office Bearer may resign from office in writing.

Disqualification or Removal: If an Office Bearer does not attend three meetings in a row, without having applied for and obtaining leave of absence from the Board, the Board will find a new member to take that person's place.

5. Powers of the Organisation

5.1 The Board shall carry out the powers on behalf of the organisation and manage its affairs in accordance with the resolutions of the members taken at General Meetings.

5.2 The Board is responsible for making decisions to achieve the objectives of the organisation, provided such decisions are not against the resolutions of members or against the law of the Republic of South Africa.

5.3 The Board shall have the general powers and authority to:

  • Raise funds or to invite and receive contributions.
  • Buy, hire or exchange for any property that it needs to achieve its objectives.
  • Make by-laws for proper governance and management of the organisation.
  • Form sub-committees as and when it is necessary for proper functioning of the organisation.

5.4 The Board may decide to set up one or more sub-committees to get work done quickly or to do an inquiry.

5.5 The Board may delegate any of its powers or functions to a sub-committee provided that delegation is reflected in the minutes, at least one Office Bearer serves in the sub-committee, there are three or more people on a sub-committee, and the sub-committee regularly reports back to the Board.

5.6 The Board must in advance approve all expenditure incurred by the sub-committee, and may revoke the delegation or amend its conditions.

6. Meetings

6.1 Annual General Meetings (AGM)
  • Stakeholders of the organisation must attend its annual general meetings.
  • The purpose of an AGM is to report back to stakeholders, make changes to the constitution, and enable members to decide on the policies of the organisation.
  • The annual general meeting must be held once every year, towards the end of the organisation's financial year.
  • The AGM deals with: agenda items, attendance and apologies, previous minutes, Chairperson's report, Treasurer's report, constitutional changes, election of office bearers, general and closing.
6.2 Special General Meetings
  • Held outside of normal or regular meetings.
  • May be called by the Board or not less than one-third of the members.
  • May be called when the Board needs the mandate or guidance of the general members on issues that require urgent attention.
6.3 Ordinary Meetings
  • Ordinary member meetings are held once a quarter and attended by the board.
  • Meetings of the Board will be held at least once a quarter or when a need arises.
6.4 Notices of Meetings
  • The Chairperson convenes meetings. The Secretary must let all Board members know the date within a reasonable time, but not less than seven (7) days before.
  • For an AGM or Special General Meeting, all members must be informed no less than fourteen (14) days before.
  • Notices must be given in writing, personally, by post or electronic communication.
  • Notices must indicate the reasons for the meeting and matters to be discussed.
6.5 Quorums
  • Quorum for all meetings shall be a simple majority (50% + 1) of relevant members expected to attend.
  • For changes to the constitution or dissolution, two thirds (2/3) of members must be present to form a quorum.
  • If a quorum is not present within fifteen minutes, the meeting must be adjourned to another date within fourteen days thereafter.
  • If no quorum is present at the reconvened meeting within fifteen minutes, the members present shall be regarded to make up a quorum.
6.6 Procedures at Meetings
  • The Board may regulate its meetings as it deems fit. The Chairperson chairs all meetings; if absent, the Vice-Chairperson chairs. If both are absent, members present elect a chairperson.
6.7 Making Decisions in Meetings
  • Where possible, decisions shall be taken by consensus. When there is no consensus, members will discuss options and then call for a vote.
  • Majority votes on an issue shall be regarded as the decision of the meeting.
  • If opposing votes are equal, the chairperson has either a second or a deciding vote.
  • All members must abide by the majority decision.
6.8 Records of Meetings
  • Proper minutes and attendance records must be kept for all meetings.
  • Minutes shall be confirmed as a true record by the next meeting and signed by the chairperson.
  • Minutes shall be kept safely and be available for members to consult.

7. Income and Property

  • The organisation will keep a record of everything it owns.
  • The organisation may not give any of its money or property to its members or the Board, except to pay a reasonable amount for work done for the organisation.
  • The Board or a member can only get money back for expenses authorized and paid on behalf of the organisation.
  • The Board or members do not have rights over things that belong to the organisation.

8. Finances and Reports

  • Bank Account: The Board must open a bank account in the name of the organisation with a registered Bank.
  • Signing: Cheques and documents requiring signature shall be signed by at least two persons authorised by the Board. The chairperson and at least two other members must sign any withdrawal.
  • Financial year-end: The financial year end shall be 31 March each year.
  • Financial Report: The Board must keep proper records and books of account. Within six months of its financial year, a report is compiled by an independent registered Accounting Officer.
  • The Treasurer is responsible for ensuring the money of the organisation is safe and accounted for, and must make regular reports to the Board.
  • If the organisation has funds to invest, they may only be invested with registered financial institutions or securities listed on a licensed stock exchange.

9. Amendments to the Constitution

  • The constitution can only be changed by a resolution agreed upon and passed by not less than two thirds (2/3) (or at least 67%) of members at an AGM or special general meeting.
  • For considering changes, a two thirds (2/3) of members must be present to form a quorum.
  • Written notices must go out not less than fourteen (14) days before the meeting, indicating the proposed changes.
  • No amendments may be made which would cause the organisation to close down or stop to function.

10. Dissolution / Closing Down

  • The organisation may dissolve if at least two thirds (2/3) of the members present and voting at a meeting convened for that purpose are in favour.
  • When closing down, the organisation must pay off all its debts. Any remaining property or money should be given to another non-profit organisation with similar objectives, as decided by the general meeting.

This constitution was approved and accepted by members of MANATHA FOUNDATION at a special (general) meeting held on 20th MARCH 2024.
